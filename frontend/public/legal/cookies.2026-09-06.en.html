1. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small file a site stores in your browser to remember something between one page and the next. Without them there would be no way to keep a session open or to remember which language you want to read in.

This is a translation provided for convenience; the Spanish version prevails in case of discrepancy.

2. First, because it is what usually worries people

Sendik does not use advertising cookies, profiling cookies, social media cookies, or third-party cookies for commercial purposes. There are no trackers. Your browsing is not shared with advertisers.

Sendik uses two cookies, and both are necessary for the site to work. They are described one by one in section 3.

3. The cookies Sendik uses

3.1 sendik_refresh — keeping you signed in

  • What it is for: keeping your session open without asking for your password on every page, and renewing it securely.
  • When it is created: only when you sign in. If you browse without an account, this cookie never exists.
  • Lifetime: up to thirty (30) days.
  • Deleted: when you sign out, or when it expires.
  • How it is protected: it is HttpOnly, so no code on the page can read it; it travels only over an encrypted connection (Secure); it is SameSite=Strict, so the browser does not send it when you arrive from another site; and it is limited to the session routes, so it is not sent with any other request.

3.2 sendik_locale — remembering your language

  • What it is for: remembering whether you chose Spanish or English.
  • Why a cookie and not something else: because the language has to be known on the server, before the page is drawn. Storing it any other way would mean the first screen arrived in the wrong language.
  • When it is created: when you change the language with the selector.
  • Lifetime: one (1) year.
  • What it contains: only es or en. Nothing that identifies you.

4. Other browser storage

These are not cookies —they are not sent to the server and do not leave your device— but they are explained here because they are storage in your browser and you are entitled to know:

  • Session storage: if you mark a favourite without being signed in, we keep that intention for as long as the tab lasts, so we can carry it out when you sign in. It is cleared when you close the tab.
  • Local browser database: if you are listing a product, the photographs you take are stored on your own device while you finish, so you do not lose them if the page closes. They are cleared when you finish or discard the listing.

5. Why we do not ask you to accept cookies

Because the two cookies in section 3 are strictly necessary to provide the service you asked for: one keeps the session you opened and the other remembers the language you chose. There are no advertising or analytics cookies to consent to.

If Sendik adds non-necessary cookies in future, your prior consent will be requested before they are set and this policy will be updated.

6. How to control them yourself

Every browser lets you view, block and delete cookies from its privacy settings. You can also sign out of Sendik, which immediately removes the cookie in section 3.1.

Please note that if you block Sendik's cookies you will not be able to stay signed in, and therefore will not be able to buy, list or use your account.

7. Site errors

Sendik uses an external service to learn about errors occurring on the site so they can be fixed. That service sets no cookies and is not used for advertising or to follow your browsing. It is declared as a processor in the Personal Data Processing Policy.

8. Personal data

The processing of any data these cookies may involve is governed by the Personal Data Processing Policy, where you will also find how to exercise your rights.
